What you get

One minute of talking a day. Four things back.

1

Your own week, as it actually went

What you worked on, who you leaned on, what took the time — against what you said on Monday you'd do. Private to you.

2

Promises nobody wrote down

Every commitment made on a call or in a corridor, with no recorded resolution and how many days it has been open. These exist in no ticket system.

3

Problems three people raised, not one

Issues several people described independently, so you act on what is corroborated instead of whoever complained loudest.

4

An honest map of what it cannot see

Which departments it can hear and which it cannot, so you know how complete the picture is before you act on it.

How it stays honest

Nobody reads your notes

Your wiki is yours — private by default, shared one page at a time, and you can take it back. Leadership never sees a raw note.

No score, ever

There is no productivity number, no rating, no league table. The moment a tool grades people, they stop speaking honestly to it.

Three independent voices, or it stays quiet

Three reports only count as three if those people had not already talked to each other. That calculation is arithmetic, not opinion.

What it costs to try

One person. One week. Nobody's permission.

No rollout, no training day, no IT project. One person talks to their phone for a minute at the end of each day and reads their own week on Friday. If it isn't worth their time, nothing else happened. The organizational picture only appears later, if enough people decide their own wiki is worth keeping.
