ERPs fail here because they impose top-down process on organizations that run on relationships and voice. Nobody fills in a form. Nobody updates a ticket. People talk, the way they already do.

Logistics & distribution

The shipment nobody owned

Warehouse knows the packaging is short. Procurement knows delivery slipped a week. Finance knows the contract has a penalty clause. Sales knows the client is about to sign a bigger deal. Four fragments, four people, one shipment, and no meeting where all four are in the room.
Convergence fires when three of those four describe the same shortage without having spoken to each other.
Manufacturing

The line stops for reasons nobody writes down

Shift handovers happen in speech and die there. The operator who knows which machine drifts on humid days tells the next operator, not a system. When they leave, the knowledge leaves with them, and the next person relearns it by breaking something.
A minute of voice per shift compiles into the operator's own wiki. It is theirs, and it stays when they don't.
Professional services

Commitments made in conversation, tracked nowhere

A consultant promises a deliverable on a call. A manager asks for a number by Friday. None of it enters a tracker, because entering it costs more than remembering it — until the week when remembering fails.
The mirror lists what you committed to and have no record of finishing. Not a task manager — a record of your own word.
